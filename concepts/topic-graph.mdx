The topic graph is the central organizing structure in Driftless. Every piece of context you capture — an architectural decision, a runbook, an explanation of how a service works — lives as a **topic**. Topics connect to each other and to the code they describe, forming a navigable graph that reflects the real shape of your system.

## What a topic contains

Each topic has five fields:

- **What** — a one-line summary of the thing being documented
- **How** — a longer explanation of implementation details
- **Decisions** — the choices made and the reasoning behind them
- **Gotchas** — edge cases, known issues, and things that have tripped people up
- **Ownership** — who is responsible and who to contact

Topics are scoped to the files or components they cover. When those files change, Driftless marks the topic stale so you know the context may no longer reflect reality.

## Topic kinds

Topics are typed. The kind tells you what category of context the topic represents.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="code-context">
    Documents how a specific piece of code works — a service, module, controller, or other unit of implementation. This is the most common kind and is typically generated or suggested by the scanner.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="decision">
    Records an architectural or design decision: what was chosen, what alternatives were considered, and why. Decisions are durable — they survive beyond the PR or Slack thread where they were made.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="roadmap">
    Captures planned work or future direction for a part of the system. Useful for giving agents and teammates context about where something is headed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="runbook">
    Step-by-step operational instructions for a process, incident response, or deployment. Keeps operational knowledge in the same graph as the code it applies to.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="customer-insight">
    Context from customer feedback, support tickets, or research that affects how a part of the system should behave. Bridges product understanding and implementation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="integration-note">
    Documents how your system integrates with a third-party service or external dependency — auth flows, webhook shapes, API quirks, and so on.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="docs-note">
    Notes about the state or gaps in your documentation. Useful for tracking what's been written, what's missing, and what's out of date.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="operational-note">
    Observations about the running system — performance characteristics, known load patterns, deployment constraints, and infrastructure considerations.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Typed relations

Topics connect to each other through typed relations. The two primary relation types are:

- **`depends_on`** — the source topic's subject directly depends on the target's subject
- **`relates_to`** — a looser association, useful for cross-cutting concerns or thematic links

You can also create connections using **wiki-links** (e.g. `[[auth-service]]`) anywhere in a topic's text. Driftless resolves these links to other topics and renders them as edges in the graph.

## The /graph dashboard

Navigate to `/graph` in your Driftless dashboard to see the full topic graph for your workspace. The graph view gives you:

- **Color-coded nodes by kind** — each topic kind has a distinct color so you can orient yourself at a glance
- **Status indicators** — a red border means the topic is stale (its covered code has changed); a dashed amber border means the topic is a draft
- **Detail panel** — click any node to see its fields, relations, and a content preview
- **Layout and filtering** — toggle between vertical and horizontal layouts, search by topic name, and filter by kind or status

The graph is a live view of your context coverage. Red nodes are where you have gaps; fresh nodes are where you are confident.
