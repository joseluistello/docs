title: "What is Driftless? Context that stays current"
description: "Driftless is a context and drift-awareness layer that keeps your team and AI agents in sync as your code evolves and decisions accumulate."
---

Driftless gives you a living layer of context on top of your codebase. You write topics — short, structured notes about what a module does, why a decision was made, how a runbook works — and Driftless watches your code. When the files a topic covers change, the topic is automatically flagged as stale so you always know what's out of date before you or your agents rely on it.

It's built for developers who work with AI coding agents, manage complex repos, or just want architectural knowledge to survive longer than a Slack thread.

## The core loop

Driftless follows a five-stage loop to keep your context fresh:

1. **Scan** — Driftless scans your repo and builds a structural map of your codebase (components, modules, patterns).
2. **Observe** — It watches PRs, pushes, and code changes over time.
3. **Contextualize** — Topics are matched to the files they cover using glob patterns and structural metadata.
4. **Deliver** — Agents and teammates pull current context before starting work.
5. **Sync** — When covered code changes, affected topics are flagged as stale so you can update them before pushing.

## Topic kinds

Every topic has a `kind` that signals its purpose. Driftless uses kind to color-code nodes in the graph and to surface the right context to agents.

| Kind | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| `code-context` | Describes what a module or component does and how it works |
| `decision` | Records an architectural or product decision and its rationale |
| `roadmap` | Tracks planned work and direction |
| `runbook` | Step-by-step operational procedures |
| `customer-insight` | Observations about user behavior or customer feedback |
| `integration-note` | Details about how external services are connected |
| `docs-note` | Notes tied to documentation or specs |
| `operational-note` | Ongoing operational concerns, incidents, or observations |

## The topic graph

Topics connect to each other through typed relations like `depends_on` and `relates_to`, and through wiki-links inside topic content. In the dashboard's architecture map, nodes are color-coded by kind. A **red border** means the topic is stale — its covered code changed. A **dashed amber border** means the topic is a draft.

<Note>
  Your source code never leaves your machine. Driftless stores structural metadata and topic text only — not raw code or secrets.
</Note>
